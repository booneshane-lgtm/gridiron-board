    How the projection works

    Each projected score starts from the market: the consensus spread sets the margin and the consensus total sets the scoring level (the market is the single best predictor and every model above leans on it). That baseline is then nudged by (1) Kalshi and Polymarket win/cover probabilities, which reflect real money rather than opinion; (2) SportsLine's 10,000-simulation model where its lean was published; (3) the College Football News 13-expert panel (an asterisk in their picks means "wins but doesn't cover," which is a strong signal on big spreads); (4) reported line movement and where the money is (e.g., Patriots money moving SEA from -4 to -3.5; Oregon total dropping 53.5 → 51.5 on public Over money). Confidence on a money line is the blended win probability. Confidence on a spread or total starts at 50% and rises only with source agreement and distance between the projection and the number — anything above ~58% on a spread or total is rare and should be. Each game carries a single Best bet: the pick with the largest edge over its break-even (52.4% for a -110 spread or total; the book's implied probability for a money line). If nothing clears break-even by at least a point, the game is marked Pass. Sharp signals — a money-vs-tickets split, reverse line movement, and steam moves — add or subtract up to four points: when the money side agrees with the projection the number goes up, when it disagrees it comes down. Venue, weather and injury notes appear in each game's detail panel; on a live refresh the page pulls ESPN's venue, forecast and NFL injury lists.

    Money lines marked est. are converted from the spread with a standard NFL/CFB curve because that book's exact price couldn't be verified at pull time. Lines move constantly — check the number at your book before doing anything with this. This board is a projection tool, not a guarantee.
